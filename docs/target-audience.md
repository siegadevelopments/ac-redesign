## 1. Primary Ideal Customer Profile

### Definition

An Australian small or medium-sized business with 10–100 employees, growing operational
complexity, and no large internal software team.

Typical decision-makers: Founder, Owner, Managing Director, CEO, Operations leader. The buyer is
usually the person who feels the operational pain directly, not a delegated IT role.

### Company Characteristics

- Growing operational complexity
- Repetitive administrative work
- Spreadsheet-heavy workflows
- Disconnected systems (CRM, booking, accounting, messaging all separate)
- Manual quoting
- Manual booking
- Manual reporting
- Repetitive customer communication
- Business has grown faster than its internal systems

### Pain Points

- Staff manually copy data between systems
- Employees spend hours on repetitive tasks
- Important information is spread across multiple systems
- Customers have difficulty booking or accessing services
- Reporting requires manual effort
- Existing software doesn't match how the business actually works
- Business processes depend heavily on individual employees remembering to do them
- Management lacks real-time visibility into operations

### Buying Triggers

- Rapid growth
- Hiring more administrative staff
- Operational inefficiency becoming visible/painful
- A new location or new product/service launch
- Multiple software systems being adopted without integration
- Spreadsheet dependency reaching a breaking point
- Increasing customer volume
- Need for internal dashboards or visibility
- Existing off-the-shelf software becoming limiting

### Buying Signals (qualification)

High-value:

- A real, specific operational problem (not "we should probably do something with AI")
- Budget authority in the room
- Clear operational pain, not hypothetical
- Existing systems to integrate with (not a greenfield "build us an app" request)
- Some timeline pressure
- Decision-making authority
- Openness to ongoing support, not just a one-off build

Low-value (deprioritize):

- Shopping purely for the cheapest developer
- One-off low-budget website requests
- No defined business value behind the request
- Unrealistic AI feature requests ("just make it fully autonomous")
- Seeking free technical consulting with no intent to engage

### Objections

**"How much does this cost?"** — Positioning: no fixed number upfront; the Free Assessment
exists specifically to scope this honestly rather than guess. See `docs/offers.md` for the
indicative range shown only post-assessment.

**"Do we actually need custom software, or can we just buy something off the shelf?"** —
Positioning: we recommend the simplest solution that solves the problem, including "buy,
don't build" when that's genuinely true. See `docs/conversion-strategy.md`.

**"Is this just another AI vendor overselling AI?"** — Positioning: AI is a mechanism, applied
only where it creates measurable value — not the pitch itself. See `docs/brand.md`'s words to
avoid.

**"Can a small, overseas-based team actually support us long-term?"** — Positioning: transparent
about team size and location (see `docs/australian-market.md`), backed by a real ongoing client
relationship and a named Care Plan for continuity.

### Desired Outcomes

The buyer isn't buying "technology." They're buying:

- Less manual work
- Faster operations
- Better visibility
- Fewer mistakes
- Better customer experience
- More capacity without more headcount
- Scalable processes
- Reduced operational friction
- Better use of employee time

## 2. Secondary ICP — Agencies (served by `/for-agencies`, not the homepage)

### Definition

Australian founder-led digital, AI, automation, and software agencies, roughly 2–30 people, that
can sell technical projects but occasionally lack the engineering capacity to deliver them.

Typical decision-makers: Founder, CEO, Managing Director, Technical Director, CTO, Head of
Delivery — usually commercially responsible for delivering client projects.

### Agency Problems

**Capacity**: too many projects, not enough developers, internal team fully booked, tight
deadlines, need for temporary engineering capacity.

**Capability**: agency can sell the project but lacks in-house expertise in backend development,
APIs, AI implementation, database architecture, complex integrations, automation, cloud
infrastructure, or custom application development.

**Delivery**: projects becoming technically complex, existing developers overloaded, rising
client expectations, need for a reliable technical partner.

### Agency Buying Triggers

New client won; large project won; existing team overloaded; a developer resigned; a client
requests AI implementation, custom software, API integration, or automation; the agency wants to
expand its technical offering or accept projects outside its internal capability.

### Agency Objections

**"Will you take my client?"** — Apprising supports the agency's client relationship rather than
competing for it.

**"Will the quality be good?"** — Engineering quality, documentation, testing, maintainability,
and professional delivery are priorities.

**"Will communication be difficult?"** — Clear technical communication, defined responsibilities,
documentation, structured delivery.

**"Are you just cheap offshore developers?"** — Never compete primarily on price. Apprising
positions as a professional engineering partner for agencies that need reliable technical
delivery capacity, not the cheapest available hands.
